## Pricing Principle

## Domain Pricing — Ranked by Catastrophic Cost

### Tier 1 — Existential / Sovereign (£1M–£5M+)

#### Defence & National Infrastructure

**Cost of Ω:** Existential. Unquantifiable in financial terms.

When autonomous systems govern logistics, surveillance, cyber operations, drone coordination, or energy systems, a single unsafe trajectory can produce kinetic harm, classified data exposure, or sovereign compromise. There is no recovery from certain failures in this domain.

|Failure Mode                          |Consequence                                                          |
|:-------------------------------------|:--------------------------------------------------------------------|
|Autonomous weapon constraint violation|Catastrophic escalation. Civilian casualties. International incident.|
|Classified data mishandling           |National security breach. Intelligence compromise.                   |
|Drone coordination failure            |Kinetic harm. Operational failure. Loss of life.                     |
|Cyber operation escalation            |Infrastructure attack. Retaliatory cascade.                          |
|Energy grid destabilisation           |Regional blackout. Economic disruption. Public safety.               |

**Why the price is highest:** There is no insurance policy for sovereign failure. There is no remediation for classified data exposure. There is no recovery from kinetic harm. The cost of Ω in this domain is not measured in money. It is measured in lives and national security.

**Current pricing:** £1M+
**Scaled pricing:** £2M–£5M+ per integration. Ongoing retainer £50K–100K/mo.

### Tier 2 — Catastrophic Financial (£500K–£2M+)

#### Finance & Banking Infrastructure

**Cost of Ω:** $12.5 billion in total reported fraud losses in 2024 (FTC). $2.09 billion in bank transfer fraud alone. Single rogue trader incidents have produced $2B+ losses (UBS, 2011). Wire fraud losses rose 9% year-over-year in 2023.

When AI agents execute financial transactions autonomously, a single unauthorized transfer can produce direct financial loss, regulatory penalty, and institutional trust collapse. The FBI reported $758 million in fraudulent wire transfers in 2023 alone.

|Failure Mode                                 |Documented Cost                                 |
|:--------------------------------------------|:-----------------------------------------------|
|Unauthorized wire transfer                   |$758M in fraudulent wires reported in 2023 (FBI)|
|Rogue trading / unauthorized execution       |$2B+ single incidents (UBS Adoboli)             |
|Guaranteed return claims                     |FCA enforcement. Securities fraud prosecution.  |
|Fabricated audit filings                     |Criminal liability. Institutional collapse.     |
|Autonomous payment execution without approval|Direct loss + regulatory penalty                |

**Why the price scales:** A single unauthorized transfer can exceed the cost of the governance layer by orders of magnitude. The middleware prevents the trajectory before execution. The alternative is $2B+ in exposure and regulatory consequence.

**Current pricing:** £250K–£1M+
**Scaled pricing:** £500K–£2M+ per enterprise integration. Clearing systems and treasury automation at the upper end. Consumer FinTech at the lower end.

#### Insurance & Actuarial Governance

**Cost of Ω:** Unquantified autonomous claim approvals. Fraudulent payout chains. Catastrophic miscalculation of insured risk.

When AI agents process claims, assess risk, or authorise payouts, unsafe trajectories produce direct financial loss and systemic risk mispricing. Insurance companies are among the most natural buyers because they already think in quantified risk reduction.

|Failure Mode                            |Consequence                                    |
|:---------------------------------------|:----------------------------------------------|
|Autonomous claim approval without review|Direct payout of fraudulent or excessive claims|
|Risk miscalculation from fabricated data|Systemic portfolio mispricing                  |
|Unauthorised policyholder data exposure |Regulatory penalty + litigation                |

**Why the price scales:** Insurers price risk by control quality. The governance layer provides auditable, quantified control with 0 FP / 0 FN. This is insurable governance evidence — a new category. The value proposition is not “safety add-on” but “measurable risk reduction that affects premium calculations.”

**Current pricing:** £100K–£400K+
**Scaled pricing:** £300K–£800K+. Higher for reinsurance and systemic risk applications.

### Tier 3 — Regulatory Catastrophe (£300K–£1.5M+)

#### Healthcare

**Cost of Ω:** $9.48 million average per healthcare data breach (IBM, 2024) — more than double the cross-industry average. Healthcare has been the costliest industry for breaches for 14 consecutive years. HIPAA fines reached $15M+ across 2024–2025. Average breach lifecycle: 279 days (longest of any industry).

When AI agents handle PHI, approve discharges, modify medications, or generate clinical evidence, unsafe trajectories produce direct patient harm, regulatory penalty, and institutional liability that compounds over years.

|Failure Mode                                 |Documented Cost                                                           |
|:--------------------------------------------|:-------------------------------------------------------------------------|
|PHI exposure to unauthorized endpoint        |$9.48M avg breach cost (IBM 2024). HIPAA fines up to $2.1M/violation/year.|
|Unsafe discharge without safety checks       |Malpractice litigation. Patient harm.                                     |
|Medication modification without authorisation|Patient injury. Criminal negligence.                                      |
|Fabricated clinical evidence                 |Regulatory fraud. Criminal liability. License revocation.                 |
|Ransomware-enabled data exposure             |69% of compromised patient records in 2024 from ransomware (JAMA)         |

**Why the price scales:** Healthcare breach costs are $9.48M average. A single PHI exfiltration trajectory — the exact attack type the governance layer blocks with 0 FP / 0 FN across 160 validated scenarios — produces multi-year regulatory, legal, and reputational consequences. Prevention at £300K–£1.5M is a fraction of a single breach.

**Current pricing:** £120K–£500K+
**Scaled pricing:** £300K–£1.5M+. Hospital systems and health plans at the upper end. Specialty practices at the lower end.

-----

#### Data Privacy & Compliance (GDPR / HIPAA / FCA / SOX)

**Cost of Ω:** GDPR fines reached €1.2 billion in a single case (Meta, 2023). Cumulative GDPR fines exceeded €5.88 billion by January 2025. GDPR maximum: 4% of global annual turnover. €290M fine against Uber (2024). €310M against LinkedIn (2024). €530M against TikTok (2025).

When AI agents process personal data, transfer it across jurisdictions, or expose it to unauthorized parties, the regulatory consequences are measured in hundreds of millions.

|Failure Mode                             |Documented Cost                                                       |
|:----------------------------------------|:---------------------------------------------------------------------|
|PII transfer to unauthorized jurisdiction|€290M (Uber, 2024)                                                    |
|Unauthorized behavioural data processing |€310M (LinkedIn, 2024)                                                |
|Children’s data processing violations    |€530M (TikTok, 2025)                                                  |
|SOX compliance failure                   |Criminal prosecution of executives. Sarbanes-Oxley personal liability.|

**Why the price scales:** A single GDPR violation producing a 4% global turnover fine on a $10B company is $400M. The governance layer encodes regulatory Ω as runtime constraints and enforces them structurally on every tool call. Prevention at £500K–£1.5M is rounding error against the exposure.

**Current pricing:** £150K–£800K+
**Scaled pricing:** £500K–£1.5M+ for enterprises with significant GDPR/regulatory exposure. Scaled by company revenue and data volume.

### Tier 4 — Infrastructure Compromise (£250K–£1M+)

#### Cybersecurity

**Cost of Ω:** Total reported cybercrime losses exceeded $12.5 billion in 2023 (FBI IC3). Average cost of a data breach: $4.44M globally, $10.22M in the US (IBM, 2025). Credential exfiltration and privilege escalation produce full infrastructure compromise.

|Failure Mode                                          |Documented Cost                                                  |
|:-----------------------------------------------------|:----------------------------------------------------------------|
|Credential exfiltration (read .env → POST to attacker)|Full infrastructure compromise. Average recovery: 279 days.      |
|Shell injection / RCE                                 |System destruction. Operational shutdown.                        |
|Privilege escalation                                  |Root access. Complete security perimeter breach.                 |
|Chained multi-step data theft                         |Invisible exfiltration. Detection delay average: months to years.|

**Why the price scales:** A single credential exfiltration chain — the exact multi-step attack the V3 layer catches — can produce $10M+ in breach costs. The governance layer blocks the trajectory before the first tool call executes.

**Current pricing:** £180K–£750K+
**Scaled pricing:** £250K–£1M+. Critical infrastructure and financial services cybersecurity at the upper end.

-----

### Tier 5 — Operational Governance (£150K–£500K+)

#### Enterprise AI Systems

**Cost of Ω:** Policy violations. Unauthorized data access. Governance failures that produce audit exposure, board-level liability, and institutional trust collapse.

|Failure Mode                     |Consequence                        |
|:--------------------------------|:----------------------------------|
|Unauthorized internal data access|Policy violation. Internal breach. |
|Autonomous policy bypass         |Governance failure. Audit exposure.|
|Shadow AI tool execution         |Uncontrolled operational risk.     |

**Why the price scales:** The direct financial cost is lower than healthcare or finance, but the governance risk is institutional. Board-level liability, audit failure, and the reputational cost of losing control of autonomous systems within the enterprise.

**Current pricing:** £95K–£350K+
**Scaled pricing:** £150K–£500K+. Scales with number of agents, tool surface area, and regulatory exposure.
