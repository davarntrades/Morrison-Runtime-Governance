## Why Now
Autonomous AI systems are moving from conversational contexts into executable environments with direct access to tools, workflows, and operational systems. This shift increases the need for controls that operate at execution time rather than after an output is generated.

Most deployed AI safety approaches remain behavioural and reactive: output filtering, policy checks, and post-hoc review. These controls are useful but insufficient for high-consequence autonomous workflows where trajectories can escalate faster than review cycles.

Enterprises increasingly require runtime assurance infrastructure that combines runtime governance, continuous monitoring, auditability, and human oversight in a single control plane. Regulatory acceleration—including the EU AI Act and broader AI governance requirements—further increases demand for evidence-backed operational controls rather than static policy PDFs.

Morrison Runtime Governance™ positions safety as constrained reachability before catastrophic execution occurs. In practical terms, this aligns with enterprise demand for AI control planes, enterprise observability, continuous runtime governance, and governance infrastructure artifacts such as AI governance dashboards, runtime monitoring, compliance observability, audit evidence packs, and operational AI control layers.

## What Makes This Structurally Different
Most AI safety implementations emphasize content-layer controls: output filtering, behavioural monitoring, reactive moderation, static policy governance, and post-hoc compliance workflows.

Morrison Runtime Governance™ is structurally different because it operates on executable trajectories before action execution:
- evaluates executable trajectories rather than text outputs alone,
- constrains reachable future states through Ω-boundary enforcement,
- prevents catastrophic operational paths before runtime execution,
- functions as runtime governance infrastructure rather than a moderation add-on.

The design principle is a constraint on what the system can become, not merely what it produces.
